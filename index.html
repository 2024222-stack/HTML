📐 Theorems & Formula Reference

Radius (r): Distance from the center point to any edge.

Diameter (d): Passes through center connecting opposite edges. d = 2r

Chord: Straight line segment whose endpoints lie on the circle boundary.

Calculations based on π = 3.14:

  • Circumference (Perimeter): C = 2 * π * r
  • Area: A = π * r²

A line from the center perpendicular to a chord always bisects that chord, forming a right-angled triangle:

r² = (Half Chord)² + (Distance from Center)²

Tangent: A line touching the circle boundary at exactly one point.

  • The angle between a radius and a tangent line at contact point is always 90°.

The standard equation for a circle centered at the origin (0,0) with radius r is:

x² + y² = r²

When a circle center shifts away from the origin to position (h, k), its equation alters into vertex form:

(x - h)² + (y - k)² = r²

Example: Centered at (2, 1) with radius 3.5 creates (x - 2)² + (y - 1)² = 12.25.

Angle at the Center Theorem: The angle subtended by an arc at the center is exactly **twice** the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining circumference.

  • Angle at Center = 2 * Angle at Circumference
  • Angles subtended by the same arc in the same segment are equal.
