📐 Detailed Theorems & Frameworks

Circles are defined as a set of points equidistant from a singular focus core called the Center.

  • Radius (r): The structural foundation vector stretching from the absolute core to the outer locus framework boundary.
  • Diameter (d): The maximal straight chord distance slicing directly across the circle framework through the center anchor. It is exactly double the radius scale.
  • Chord: Any line entry bridging two distinct boundary locus coordinates together without needing to intersect the center.
Diameter Equation: d = 2r

Quantifying circle geometry utilizes the transcendental constant pi (π ≈ 3.14159), representing the ratio of any circle's perimeter to its core diameter path.

  • Circumference (C): The total metric distance wrapping around the external boundary rim edge profile.
  • Area (A): The complete dimensional space enclosed inside the circular perimeter framework boundary.

Adjusting the radius slider exponentially scales the interior space area compared to linear perimeter changes.

C = 2πr
A = πr²

This theorem provides a vital bridge between standard circular properties and linear trigonometry profiles:

When a line is drawn from the center of a circle perpendicular (at 90°) to a chord, it will always split that chord into two perfectly equal halves (bisecting it).

This layout creates a right-angled triangle profile inside the shape. By isolating half of the sliced chord, you can use the Pythagorean Theorem to find missing values:

r² = (Half Chord)² + (Distance from Center)²

A Tangent line is a linear geometric vector passing along the circle exterior that strikes the boundary curve at exactly one coordinate point without slicing through the interior.

  • The 90° Contact Rule: A radius line meeting a tangent line at its exact point of contact will always intersect at a perpendicular 90° angle.

Use the slider to spin the point of contact around the circle to observe how the tangent line constantly maintains its perpendicular orientation relative to the radius.

On a 2D Cartesian plane layout, a circle represents an collection of points matching specific coordinate conditions calculated using distance frameworks.

When fixed with its center directly over the origin coordinate (0,0), the relation simplifies into standard form derived via basic Pythagorean tracking:

x² + y² = r²

Every point (x, y) sitting precisely on the outer edge boundary will satisfy this algebraic criteria.

When a circle's center coordinates shift away from the origin to a new structural coordinate designated as (h, k), the spatial tracking formula shifts accordingly.

This translation updates the algebraic structure into vertex form:

(x - h)² + (y - k)² = r²

Use the h and k sliders to change the center position. You will see the coordinate map update and track the spatial transformation across the coordinate grid lines.

This foundational theorem dictates structural angular distribution properties across shared circular arc lengths:

Angle at the Center Theorem: The angle subtended by an arc at the center point is exactly twice (2x) the size of the angle subtended by that same identical arc length extending to any point along the remaining circumference boundary profile.

Angle at Center = 2 * Angle at Circumference

Slide the arc width slider to expand or contract the boundaries, and observe how the two angles scale proportionally.
